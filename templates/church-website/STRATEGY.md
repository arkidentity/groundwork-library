# How we build church websites that convert

Most church sites fail the same way: they are an **archive for members**, organized by the
church's org chart (About, Ministries, Staff, Sermons), with the newcomer's questions buried.
Nobody who might visit gets a clear next step.

## What "convert" means for a church
The conversion is **a first visit, planned.** Not a form fill. The site succeeds when a stranger
can go from "I wonder about this church" to "I know what Sunday is like, I know where to park, and
I told someone I'm coming (or I'm just going to show up)."

Secondary conversions, in order: a call or text, a directions tap, a ministry sign-up, giving,
sermon plays. Members and givers matter, but they already know where to go, so they never get the
front door.

## The principles
1. **One job per page, one button per job.** Every page has a single next step.
2. **Plan Your Visit is the homepage.** Time, address, and one button above the fold. Below it, answer the
   fears in the order visitors have them: What happens? What do I wear? Where do my kids go?
   Will I be singled out? What if I don't know when to stand or sit?
3. **Show people, not the building.** Real recent photos of real people. The building only so they
   recognize it. No stock.
4. **Match reality.** Whatever the site promises must be what Sunday is. The site is a first
   impression of a real room.
5. **Say who you are in plain words, early.** Denomination and what it means in a few lines, no
   insider vocabulary. If they have to Google "ELCA," we lost them.
6. **Make contact easy, not formal.** Call and text buttons beat a contact form. Forms are the
   least used page.
7. **A person, a face, a welcome.** A short welcome video or a named greeter. People visit
   people.
8. **Serve the local search first.** Most first visits start on Google Maps or "church near me."
   The Google Business Profile (hours, photos, link, reviews) matters as much as the site. Name,
   address and phone must match everywhere.
9. **Mobile first, fast, current.** A stale events page says "nobody's home." If nobody will update
   it, don't put it in.
10. **Members get their own quiet corner.** Calendar, bulletin, giving, serving: one click from the
    footer or a "Members" link, not in the hero.

## Two audiences, two front doors
Every church has **visitors** (want to know if it's safe and what it's like) and **people who
already belong** (want times, calendar, giving). Design the homepage for the first; give the
second fast links. If the church has a second public audience (for example, parents at the church's
Center), it gets its own path, not a paragraph.

## What we measure (set up before launch)
| Signal | How |
|---|---|
| Planned visits | "Tell us you're coming" submissions, or call/text taps |
| Directions taps, call taps, text taps | click events on those buttons |
| Sunday card: "How did you hear about us?" | a line on the visitor card, tallied weekly |
| Google Business Profile views, calls, direction requests | GBP insights |
| Sermon plays, give clicks | secondary |

Report one number monthly: **planned visits**, and the trend. Set expectations honestly: people look at
a church about a dozen times online before they show up, so the trend takes months and the site is
not a guarantee. Promise the trend, never a headcount.

## Deciding what to build
- **Start small and true.** A church of 40 with no youth ministry needs a 6-page site that's
  honest, not a 25-page site with empty sections.
- **Every page we don't build is a page nobody has to keep current.**
- **Copy comes from their real words.** See Module B (messaging). Without it, use their own
  sentences from calls and answers, lightly edited, never invented.
