# Language audit rubric (team only)

Run once the client's material and at least two interview conversations are in. Source it from
their live copy (website, ads, sales script) **and** what they actually say (answers, call
transcript). The gap between the two is often the finding.

**Flags and reasons only. No replacement lines.** Replacements come in the messaging work itself,
after the harvest is done.

## What to flag

| Flag | What it looks like | Why it hurts |
|---|---|---|
| **The company as the hero** | "We're the top…", "our commitment to excellence", "family-owned since…" up front | The customer is looking for someone to solve *their* problem. A story about the company makes them do the work of finding themselves in it. |
| **Claims a stranger can't believe** | top, best, #1, quality, 100% satisfaction, premier, trusted | Everyone says them, so they read as noise. Unprovable claims also cost trust with careful buyers. |
| **Leading with what you're not** | "no pressure," "no hidden fees," "not like other companies" | Once, it reassures. As the whole brand, it names the customer's fear and leaves them there. |
| **Industry words** | trade terms, product codes, certifications as headlines | The customer doesn't search for or say these. They signal expertise to peers, not to buyers. |
| **Vague outcomes** | "transform your home," "peace of mind," "solutions" | Nothing a customer can picture. |
| **Too many offers** | every product and service listed at the same weight | The reader can't tell what to do first. |
| **Weak or missing call to action** | "learn more," "contact us," five different buttons | No clear next step, so people don't take one. |
| **Promises they can't keep yet** | trust claims out ahead of reviews, capacity or track record | The first bad review turns the promise into the evidence against them. |
| **Their own voice missing** | the site sounds nothing like how they talk on a call | Their real words are usually warmer and clearer. |

## Also note what's working
- Lines that already sound like them and a customer would repeat
- Promises they can actually prove
- Words customers use in reviews that the copy doesn't use yet

## Output

1. **Team version** (`messaging/language-audit.md`): every flag with the source quote, the rule it
   breaks and the evidence. This feeds the locked and banned word lists.
2. **Client version** (`outbox/shared/messaging-checkup.md`, plus a review item that links it):
   "What's working and what's getting in your way." Plain language, warm, 3 to 6 flags, most
   important first, each with the quote and one or two sentences on why. Start with what's
   working. Anything sensitive (reputation, reviews) is left for a conversation, never written.
